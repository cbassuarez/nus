B · Wire

A rule draws to where it’s kept. Nothing flies. From your click a 2 px signal rule drops to the window’s foot and runs to the corner where downloads live. Then the foot itself is the progress: it fills toward the far side as bytes arrive, and when the file is done it draws back into the corner and leaves a dot. This is Broadsheet’s own motion (rules extend), on the edge opposite the page-load bar.

To build. A foot rule, with its fill driven by the totals in DOWNLOADS, plus the corner notch. There’s no new layer and nothing to hit-test; it retracts on the load bar’s LOAD_OUT. The smallest of the three, about half a day.
How it reads. Click, and a red line connects the link to the bottom-left corner, the corner where downloads live. The line stays as a hairline progress bar along the foot of the window, readable from anywhere without opening anything. At 100% it draws back into the corner, and a dot stays there until you look.
Motion. The drop is 140 ms and the run along the foot is Motion::travel(ROW × 2); both are rules growing, which anim.rs already allows. The progress fill is linear in bytes, not time. It retracts on LOAD_OUT (220 ms base), the same curve the load bar uses.
For. Nothing covers the page. Progress is always visible, with no list and no hover: the old Chrome shelf without the shelf. It’s the quietest of the three and the most native. It works the same with the sidebar on the right (it runs right).
Against. A 4 KB file finishes before the run lands, so the draw has to play out anyway or the signal is lost. A rule says “connected to that corner”, not “a file”, so it’s weaker on first use. Two downloads share one foot: the fill shows the total, and the words name the newest.
