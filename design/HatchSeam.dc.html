A · Seam

Only the lip. Open, the hatch has a lip along its foot that you drag. Closed, a short length of that lip stays at the top edge where the sheet comes down: a 5 px seam that says everything with one line. Each piece of work is a segment. A shuttle runs along the ones that are going, a pendant hangs under one that needs you, a dot under one that finished, and one that failed is hatched. Reach for it and it thickens into a handle; pull, and the hatch follows your pointer.

The loop. 0 s: two segments, a lane filling and a shuttle running. 3 s: fly deploy joins and a pendant drops. 6 s: cargo test finishes and its slip drops (2.6 s here, 6 s in the app). 8.8 s: you reach for it.
How it reads. From across the screen you can tell how many things are up there (segments), whether any are moving (a shuttle) and whether one wants you (something hangs down). With nothing running and nus in the background it’s one short, empty seam. With nus in front, it’s nothing at all.
Motion. The shuttle, a 7 px dash, crosses its segment in 2.4 s (ease in-out) and rests 0.6 s. The pendant breathes (opacity 1 → .35 over 2.4 s, in phase with any other). Hover grows the seam on ROW. Under reduced motion a running segment is a still half-fill and nothing breathes.
Footprint. 5 px tall and 30–104 px wide at rest. The window is 12 px tall so it’s easy to hit, and it takes clicks only on what’s drawn (+3 px). Today’s badge is 330 × 30.
For. It’s the smallest thing that can still say all of it. Open and closed are one object, so it teaches itself: you pull the lip down to open the hatch, the way you drag it to resize. Dark when normal.
Against. The most abstract of the three: it has to be learned, and past five or six segments you can’t count them. 5 px can get lost on a busy tab strip, and a 5 px pendant is a quiet way to say “needs you”.
To build. Segments, shuttle and pendant in hatch_badge_frame. CursorMoved/Left and press-drag on the badge window, which takes only a release today. The drag drives the sheet’s slide. Per-segment tooltips and the notice slip. About a day and a half.
