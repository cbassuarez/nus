How it reads. One page holds everything kept, ruled like the ports board. Lenses across the top are the roles and the collections; the reading list is the to read lens, not a second page. The right column is the item: what it is, where it sits, what it's for, its copy, and the notes that cite it.
For. Room for 341 items and for tidying them; the reading-list page already exists to grow from. Search reaches the kept copies' text, so you find a page by what it said.
Against. A page is a place you have to go. On its own it becomes the graveyard most bookmark managers are: filed once, never visited. It needs the palette and the address field to bring things back.
