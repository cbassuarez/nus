Common ground

What all three share. The states a tab can be in, where nus already reads each one, and one ladder of loudness. The directions differ in how a row says it: a fixed cell (A), the tab itself (B), or words when there's news (C).

One ladder. A tab shows its loudest state; a stack, the loudest of its members; the window's strip counts them. Waits beats failed beats running beats done beats playing. Idle is silence, not a word.
Seen. 'Unseen' ends when the tab has been in front for a second, or its row hovered for one. Failed stays until seen; done fades. A tab nobody looked at never pretends to be fine.
Not colour alone. Every state has a shape or a word as well as a colour (solid, hatched, dashes, outline; or the word itself), so it reads in monochrome, for colour blindness, and in the 48px column. The row's accessible name carries the state first: "claude, waits for you: bash, cargo test."
Calm. Only running moves, and slowly; nothing blinks. The clock ticks once a second. Reduced motion: no walking dashes, the clock still counts.
To add. 'Unseen' needs a per-tab seen-at; 'listening' needs ports mapped to a tab's process tree; a stack's roll-up is new. The rest is read today.
