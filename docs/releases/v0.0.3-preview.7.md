# nus 0.0.3 preview 7

## Release summary

- The hatch comes in from the side. On Windows and Linux the hatch is now a drawer from the edge of the screen away from your tabs: tabs on the left, it comes in from the right; put the tabs on the right (Sidebar · Side of the window) and it comes in from the left, at once if it is pinned open. It runs the height of the screen above the taskbar, Size sets how far it comes out (a share of the screen's width), and its lip runs down its inner edge to drag it wider or narrower. Closed, its tab of lamps hangs from the same edge. The floating card is still centred, and on a Mac the hatch still drops from the top.

## Validation and boundaries

The native-app tests pass on Windows (730 passed, four explicitly ignored),
including two new ones for the drawer's geometry: flush with the work
area's edge on the side away from the tabs, clear of a taskbar along the
foot or docked on that side, never narrower than 360 px nor wider than the
work area allows. The root workspace's formatting check passes.

The placement was checked from inside a Windows release build through the
app's own shot driver (scripted steps on its event loop, captures of its
own frames, no OS input), on a 1920 × 1080 display at 125 % with the
taskbar along the foot, for both sides of the sidebar: where the drawer
should go, where Windows actually put its window once open (x 1152–1920 and
0–768, y 20–1000), and the closed tab against the same edge at the
drawer's middle. Captures of the closed tab (ready, lamps, more than six,
a hovered lamp's tooltip, a completion notice) and of the open drawer were
looked at on both edges.

Not exercised: dragging the lip by hand, the reveal by eye, a taskbar
docked on the side, more than one monitor, Linux (the work area comes from
`_NET_WORKAREA` on X11; on Wayland the compositor places the window), and
hovering or clicking the tab with a real pointer. The macOS path is
unchanged and was only compiled by CI.

The Release workflow builds and tests macOS arm64, Windows x86-64 and Linux
x86-64, then exercises packaged browser rendering and native failure recovery.
Publication requires every platform job to pass. Windows packages and the
installer require Azure signatures and install/update/uninstall checks. macOS
remains an ad-hoc signed preview while notarization credentials are incomplete.
The published workflow and `release.json` establish the actual package revision,
checksums and signing outcome.
